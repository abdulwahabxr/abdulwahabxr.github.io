Case studies

EdTech · Enterprise Training · Cross-platform AR

Visual 3D

Challenge

EdTech platforms needed high-fidelity 3D product content accessible on phones for learners across manufacturing and healthcare. Existing solutions required special hardware, broke under real-world lighting conditions, and had painful setup processes. Learners abandoned apps before reaching educational content.

Solution

Built a native 3D product viewer for iOS and Android with AR placement capabilities. Users authenticate once, browse a curated model catalog, and place products in their physical space at real scale. Intelligent caching ensures models download once, persistent across sessions. Offline support for field teams without reliable connectivity.

My Role

Sole developer. Owned full stack from architecture to app store deployment. Conducted user research with enterprise training teams, translated requirements into technical specs, built and shipped to production, and managed post-launch support. Coordinated with platform stakeholders on certification and platform-specific optimization.

Architecture

AR Foundation abstracts iOS/Android differences for plane detection and lighting estimation. Model rendering uses device-specific compression formats (USDZ, glTF) chosen at runtime. Token-based auth with silent refresh handles session management. Addressables system manages asset lifecycle and memory efficiently. SQLite stores metadata locally; REST API syncs catalog updates.

Features

Real-time AR placement with device-native physics. Model scaling and rotation controls optimized for touch. Batch model loading with progress indication. Screenshot capture for learner documentation. Dark mode support. Accessibility: full VoiceOver support, color contrast WCAG AA compliant. Offline-first architecture with automatic sync when connection restores.

Results

Live in production on the App Store and Google Play. Deployed to 1,500+ users across 370 instructors and 1,200+ students, with 12+ releases shipped across both platforms since launch. Built and maintained solo, from the auth system through app store submission and post-launch fixes.

Lessons Learned

Offline-first architecture matters more than it seems upfront—connectivity in the field isn't reliable. Device variance across the Android install base is the real cost of mobile AR; a feature working on one device doesn't mean it works everywhere. Batch model loading with clear progress indication cut down on perceived hangs during downloads. Owning the whole stack solo meant no handoff friction, but also no second pair of eyes—code review discipline had to be self-imposed.
